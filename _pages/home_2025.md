    <h2>Introduction</h2>
  </div>
</div>
<div class="row">
  <div class="col-xs-12">
    <p>
    The Workshop on Human-Robot Contact and Manipulation (HRCM 2025) at RSS 2025 aims to to unite expertise across control theory, shared autonomy, physical modeling, and human understanding to have robots operate in direct or indirect physical contact with people. 
    The workshop topics include (but are not limited to):
    </p>
    <ul>
      <li>Compliant and/or soft robot hardware design</li>
      <li>Compliant control and physical safety</li>
      <li>Collaborative manipulation and shared control</li>
      <li>Contact modeling and simulation for rigid and soft bodies</li>
      <li>Tactile sensing and haptic interfaces</li>
      <li>Planning, control, and/or learning in contact-rich environments</li>
      <li>Applications involving direct human-robot contact, such as:
        <ul>
          <li>Assisted Activities for Daily Living (ADLs), e.g., feeding, bathing, walking</li>
          <li>Exoskeletons and active prostheses</li>
          <li>Medicine, e.g., patient transfer and surgery</li>
        </ul>
      </li>
      <li>Benchmarks and metrics for HRCM applications</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div> <br>

    <h2>Workshop Format</h2>
    <p>This full-day workshop will be divided into three sections with the following themes:
    </p>
    <ul>
      <li><b>Session 1: Compliance and Control Through Contact</b> includes the development of both algorithms and hardware that enable planning, control, and safe operation of robots in direct contact with their environment or other agents.</li>
        <ul>
          <li>Breakout Discussion Topic: Create a wish list of models, simulations, datasets, algorithms, etc. that will yield improvements (e.g. more robust, more tractable) in the compliant control settings defined in Session 1.</li>
        </ul>
      <li><b>Session 2: Contact Modeling and Simulation</b> covers the development and use of rigid, deformable, and soft-body contact models and simulations, as well as sim-to-real transfer and tactile sensing.</li>
        <ul>
          <li>Breakout Discussion Topic: How can the models and simulations discussed in Session 2 (or modifications thereof) address the wish list created in Session 1?</li>
        </ul>
        <li><b>Session 3: Applications in pHRI</b> encompass all human-centric use cases of the above themes, such as human augmentation, rehabilitation, and medical devices.</li>
        <ul>
          <li>Breakout Discussion Topic: With consideration of the pHRI applications discussed in Session 3, what standardized benchmarks and metrics can be used to measure the success of the algorithms, hardware, etc. discussed in Sessions 1 and 2?</li>
        </ul>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div> <br>
